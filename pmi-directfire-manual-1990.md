## I. SYSTEM DESCRIPTION AND SPECIFICATIONS

### A. GENERAL DESCRIPTION

Congratulations! You have just purchased the state of the art in high performance automotive ignition systems—**DIRECT FIRE IGNITION**. DIRECT FIRE IGNITION (DFI) finally disposes of the archaic mechanical components that have limited the performance of automotive ignitions for the past 75 years. DFI is an advanced design that combines the functions of distributor, CD and coil into one, completely integrated system.

At the heart of DFI is an electronic distributor that fires plugs **directly** from multiple coils. Multiple coils eliminate the dwell limitations of conventional ignition systems **AND EVEN MORE IMPORTANTLY**, the need for a mechanical distributor. Gone forever are the inaccuracies of gear drives, cam actuators, breaker points, and mechanical advance mechanisms. High voltage spark distribution through open spark gaps is eliminated, along with its inherent misfires and energy losses. With DFI, each of three dual coils fires two cylinders simultaneously—one cylinder fires on compression, the other, 360 degrees away, fires on exhaust. Dual coils firing two cylinders simultaneously halve the number of coils required, while providing the added benefit of keeping plugs clean during the exhaust stroke.

With DFI, timing is established right at the crankshaft, not at the end of a gear train. The firing event is initiated by a magnetic sensor reading a high resolution, 60 tooth, crank-mounted trigger wheel. A gap of two teeth on the wheel enables DFI to re-synchronize with TDC on each crank revolution. The ultra-precise signal that results is processed through electronic logic circuitry that controls current flow and dwell to optimize the output of the coils.

Each firing event is a straight-line electrical function all the way from crankshaft trigger to spark plug! There are no mechanical linkages or open gap spark transfers to distort the signal. Timing accuracy is maintained within 0.5 degrees even at the highest engine acceleration and RPM!

DFI can deliver up to 70,000 volts on demand to fire even the widest plug gaps under the highest cylinder pressure conditions. More than 100 millijoules of energy is available at the coil outputs from cranking to 7500 RPM—that's two to three times most CD units!

Every spark is super long duration—over 1000 microseconds from idle to 7500 RPM. Even at 8000 RPM, burn duration is equivalent to 45 degrees of crank rotation. You will note that the popular "long duration, multiple-fire" ignitions on the market today don't say much about spark duration at high RPM. That's because they don't multiple-fire in the upper rev ranges, and 30–40 microseconds duration is about as good as they get.

Comparing DFI's spark to that of ordinary ignitions is like comparing a flamethrower to a match. DFI can combust marginal mixtures that most other ignitions cannot.

Advance curve and rev limit are **electronically** controlled and easily dialed in—even while the engine is running! Optional vacuum advance and boost retard transducers provide additional flexibility in tailoring advance curves to a wide variety of engine configurations and operating conditions.

There has never before been an ignition system with the performance and features of DFI!!!

### B. SPECIFICATIONS

* **OPERATING RANGE:** From 30 to 12,500 RPM
* **TIMING ACCURACY:** Maintained within ±0.5° from idle to 12,500 RPM. Spark scatter is virtually eliminated.
* **SPARK VOLTAGE:** Up to 70,000 volts on demand fires even the highest cylinder pressures and widest plug gaps.
* **SPARK DURATION:** Over 1000 microseconds from idle to 7500 RPM (Thirty times as long as typical CD units at high RPM!). Burn time is equivalent to 45° of crank rotation at 8000 RPM!
* **SPARK ENERGY:** More than 100 millijoules output from coil secondaries between cranking and 7500 RPM—more than 120 millijoules under most operating conditions (Two to three times typical CD systems.)
* **REV LIMIT:** Dial adjustable from 5000 to 8000 RPM or OFF—soft action retards timing to static setting when limit is exceeded.
* **POWER REQUIREMENTS:** 10 amp circuit on nominal 12 volt negative ground system. Operating range 8 to 18 volts (performance specs above based on 13.5 volt source).
* **TEMPERATURE LIMITS:** -30°F to +250°F.
* **EMISSIONS:** DFI technology will clean the exhaust of most performance engines, but has **NOT** been certified for use on public roads with vehicles subject to emission control laws.

## II. SENSOR INSTALLATION FOR 911 W/O AIR CONDITIONING (DF-1 & DF-2)

### A. INSTALLING THE TRIGGER WHEEL *(See Figures 1 & 2)*

1. Shift gearbox into neutral. Turn engine over until TDC mark on crank pulley (identified by "Z1" stamped on pulley) lines up with timing mark at base of fanhousing. It makes no difference if #1 cylinder is on compression or exhaust stroke—DFI doesn't care.
2. Shift gearbox into fifth gear and apply handbrake securely.
3. Loosen 19mm bolt holding crank pulley to crankshaft. Do not remove yet.  
   > **NOTE:** These bolts can be hard to break loose—a long breaker bar may be required.
4. Shift gearbox into neutral. Return engine to TDC alignment if it moved during loosening. Loosen or remove fan belt.
5. With bolt still in place, attempt to rotate pulley with your fingers—it should not budge since it is pinned to the crankshaft. If it moves, remove the pulley and inspect the locating pin. If it is loose, bent or partially sheared (not uncommon), replace the pin with a new hardened steel item before proceeding. Even a small amount of freeplay can equate to 3–4 degrees error in basic timing.
6. Install the DFI trigger wheel on the crank pulley as follows:
   * **a.** Remove the 12mm x 30mm crank pulley bolt, but not the pulley.
   * **b.** Place trigger wheel up against pulley. The large, protruding diameter of the steel bushing pressed into the trigger wheel should be facing the crank pulley.  
     > **IMPORTANT:** When properly installed, the two dots stamped in the trigger wheel to locate TDC will be facing rearward.
   * **c.** Use longer 12mm x 35mm bolt and large flat washer supplied with kit to retain trigger wheel and crank pulley. OEM wavy washer should be between bolt head and large, new washer. Apply Loctite and hand tighten bolt.  
     > **IMPORTANT:** The large flat washer must be in contact with the trigger wheel to prevent rotation of trigger wheel relative to crank pulley once tightened.
7. Align trigger wheel with crank pulley as follows to set static timing:
   * **a. START POINT FOR ALL APPLICATIONS:** Align trailing edge of TDC tooth on trigger wheel with TDC mark on pulley. The TDC tooth will be the 11th from the toothless gap on the wheel, counting in a counter-clockwise direction, and will be stamped with two dots. The trigger wheel is now aligned with TDC on the crankshaft.
   * **b. FOR 6° BTC STATIC TIMING** *(recommended for mild cams, e.g., CIS, T, E, Crane G20/G40)*: Rotate trigger wheel one tooth clockwise from TDC alignment. This will align trailing edge of 12th tooth from gap with TDC mark on crank pulley.
   * **c. FOR 9° BTC STATIC TIMING** *(recommended for long overlap cams, e.g., S, C6, Crane G60/G80)*: Rotate trigger wheel 1-1/2 teeth clockwise from TDC alignment. This will align the center of the 13th tooth from the toothless gap with the TDC mark on the crank pulley (again counting teeth in a counterclockwise direction). The two punch marks will be 1-1/2 teeth to the right of TDC.
8. Torque crank pulley retaining bolt to 120 ft-lbs, making sure that the trigger wheel remains properly aligned with the TDC mark on the crank pulley. A straight-handled box end wrench—like the fan pulley wrench from your tool kit—is preferable to an offset handled combination wrench. The offset variety will put a bending load on the bolt and may deflect it into an out-of-round condition. Applying 200 lbs force to the end of a 7" long fan pulley wrench will provide the proper torque.  
   > **CAUTION:** Be aware that some crankshafts are bored/tapped short—check for bolt bottoming out before finalizing installation.  
   *Now replace/tighten fan belt before proceeding further.*

### B. INSTALLING THE TRIGGER WHEEL SENSOR *(See Figures 3 & 4)*

1. Remove lower two bolts in center of motor mount and set aside.
2. Mount Sensor Bracket to front side of engine mount (between motor mount and fan housing) using original bolts, but adding flat washers between bolt heads and aluminum bracket. Hand tighten.
3. Use fore-aft adjustment to center sensor over trigger wheel to within 0.010". Tighten 10mm bolts securing sensor to bracket.
4. Using feeler gauge, set air gap between sensor and tip of trigger wheel teeth to 0.010" ± 0.005". **THIS DIMENSION IS CRITICAL—DON'T FAKE IT!**  
   **SUGGESTIONS:**
   * **a.** Make sure the center of any tooth lines up with TDC mark on fan housing before starting—do **not** put one of the low points between teeth on TDC.
   * **b.** Get adjustment close, and then put about 5 ft-lbs on one engine mount/bracket bolt. Leave the other bolt hand tight.
   * **c.** Get the final adjustment in spec by moving the loose side.
   * **d.** Check for out-of-round condition by measuring air gap at 90°, 180°, and 270°. If measurements exceed tolerance, go back and review trigger wheel installation procedures.
5. Tighten engine mount/bracket bolts to 35 ft-lbs and recheck air gap. Adjust as required.
6. Route the short gray sensor cable lead rearward through the cutout in the center of the engine mount. *(PROCEED TO SECTION IV)*
