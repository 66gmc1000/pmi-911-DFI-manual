## I. SYSTEM DESCRIPTION AND SPECIFICATIONS

### A. GENERAL DESCRIPTION

Congratulations! You have just purchased the state of the art in high performance automotive ignition systems—**DIRECT FIRE IGNITION**. DIRECT FIRE IGNITION (DFI) finally disposes of the archaic mechanical components that have limited the performance of automotive ignitions for the past 75 years. DFI is an advanced design that combines the functions of distributor, CD and coil into one, completely integrated system.

At the heart of DFI is an electronic distributor that fires plugs **directly** from multiple coils. Multiple coils eliminate the dwell limitations of conventional ignition systems **AND EVEN MORE IMPORTANTLY**, the need for a mechanical distributor. Gone forever are the inaccuracies of gear drives, cam actuators, breaker points, and mechanical advance mechanisms. High voltage spark distribution through open spark gaps is eliminated, along with its inherent misfires and energy losses. With DFI, each of three dual coils fires two cylinders simultaneously—one cylinder fires on compression, the other, 360 degrees away, fires on exhaust. Dual coils firing two cylinders simultaneously halve the number of coils required, while providing the added benefit of keeping plugs clean during the exhaust stroke.

With DFI, timing is established right at the crankshaft, not at the end of a gear train. The firing event is initiated by a magnetic sensor reading a high resolution, 60 tooth, crank-mounted trigger wheel. A gap of two teeth on the wheel enables DFI to re-synchronize with TDC on each crank revolution. The ultra-precise signal that results is processed through electronic logic circuitry that controls current flow and dwell to optimize the output of the coils.

Each firing event is a straight-line electrical function all the way from crankshaft trigger to spark plug! There are no mechanical linkages or open gap spark transfers to distort the signal. Timing accuracy is maintained within 0.5 degrees even at the highest engine acceleration and RPM!

DFI can deliver up to 70,000 volts on demand to fire even the widest plug gaps under the highest cylinder pressure conditions. More than 100 millijoules of energy is available at the coil outputs from cranking to 7500 RPM—that's two to three times most CD units!

Every spark is super long duration—over 1000 microseconds from idle to 7500 RPM. Even at 8000 RPM, burn duration is equivalent to 45 degrees of crank rotation. You will note that the popular "long duration, multiple-fire" ignitions on the market today don't say much about spark duration at high RPM. That's because they don't multiple-fire in the upper rev ranges, and 30–40 microseconds duration is about as good as they get.

Comparing DFI's spark to that of ordinary ignitions is like comparing a flamethrower to a match. DFI can combust marginal mixtures that most other ignitions cannot.

Advance curve and rev limit are **electronically** controlled and easily dialed in—even while the engine is running! Optional vacuum advance and boost retard transducers provide additional flexibility in tailoring advance curves to a wide variety of engine configurations and operating conditions.

There has never before been an ignition system with the performance and features of DFI!!!

### B. SPECIFICATIONS

* **OPERATING RANGE:** From 30 to 12,500 RPM
* **TIMING ACCURACY:** Maintained within ±0.5° from idle to 12,500 RPM. Spark scatter is virtually eliminated.
* **SPARK VOLTAGE:** Up to 70,000 volts on demand fires even the highest cylinder pressures and widest plug gaps.
* **SPARK DURATION:** Over 1000 microseconds from idle to 7500 RPM (Thirty times as long as typical CD units at high RPM!). Burn time is equivalent to 45° of crank rotation at 8000 RPM!
* **SPARK ENERGY:** More than 100 millijoules output from coil secondaries between cranking and 7500 RPM—more than 120 millijoules under most operating conditions (Two to three times typical CD systems.)
* **REV LIMIT:** Dial adjustable from 5000 to 8000 RPM or OFF—soft action retards timing to static setting when limit is exceeded.
* **POWER REQUIREMENTS:** 10 amp circuit on nominal 12 volt negative ground system. Operating range 8 to 18 volts (performance specs above based on 13.5 volt source).
* **TEMPERATURE LIMITS:** -30°F to +250°F.
* **EMISSIONS:** DFI technology will clean the exhaust of most performance engines, but has **NOT** been certified for use on public roads with vehicles subject to emission control laws.

## II. SENSOR INSTALLATION FOR 911 W/O AIR CONDITIONING (DF-1 & DF-2)

### A. INSTALLING THE TRIGGER WHEEL *(See Figures 1 & 2)*

1. Shift gearbox into neutral. Turn engine over until TDC mark on crank pulley (identified by "Z1" stamped on pulley) lines up with timing mark at base of fanhousing. It makes no difference if #1 cylinder is on compression or exhaust stroke—DFI doesn't care.
2. Shift gearbox into fifth gear and apply handbrake securely.
3. Loosen 19mm bolt holding crank pulley to crankshaft. Do not remove yet.  
   > **NOTE:** These bolts can be hard to break loose—a long breaker bar may be required.
4. Shift gearbox into neutral. Return engine to TDC alignment if it moved during loosening. Loosen or remove fan belt.
5. With bolt still in place, attempt to rotate pulley with your fingers—it should not budge since it is pinned to the crankshaft. If it moves, remove the pulley and inspect the locating pin. If it is loose, bent or partially sheared (not uncommon), replace the pin with a new hardened steel item before proceeding. Even a small amount of freeplay can equate to 3–4 degrees error in basic timing.
6. Install the DFI trigger wheel on the crank pulley as follows:
   * **a.** Remove the 12mm x 30mm crank pulley bolt, but not the pulley.
   * **b.** Place trigger wheel up against pulley. The large, protruding diameter of the steel bushing pressed into the trigger wheel should be facing the crank pulley.  
     > **IMPORTANT:** When properly installed, the two dots stamped in the trigger wheel to locate TDC will be facing rearward.
   * **c.** Use longer 12mm x 35mm bolt and large flat washer supplied with kit to retain trigger wheel and crank pulley. OEM wavy washer should be between bolt head and large, new washer. Apply Loctite and hand tighten bolt.  
     > **IMPORTANT:** The large flat washer must be in contact with the trigger wheel to prevent rotation of trigger wheel relative to crank pulley once tightened.
7. Align trigger wheel with crank pulley as follows to set static timing:
   * **a. START POINT FOR ALL APPLICATIONS:** Align trailing edge of TDC tooth on trigger wheel with TDC mark on pulley. The TDC tooth will be the 11th from the toothless gap on the wheel, counting in a counter-clockwise direction, and will be stamped with two dots. The trigger wheel is now aligned with TDC on the crankshaft.
   * **b. FOR 6° BTC STATIC TIMING** *(recommended for mild cams, e.g., CIS, T, E, Crane G20/G40)*: Rotate trigger wheel one tooth clockwise from TDC alignment. This will align trailing edge of 12th tooth from gap with TDC mark on crank pulley.
   * **c. FOR 9° BTC STATIC TIMING** *(recommended for long overlap cams, e.g., S, C6, Crane G60/G80)*: Rotate trigger wheel 1-1/2 teeth clockwise from TDC alignment. This will align the center of the 13th tooth from the toothless gap with the TDC mark on the crank pulley (again counting teeth in a counterclockwise direction). The two punch marks will be 1-1/2 teeth to the right of TDC.
8. Torque crank pulley retaining bolt to 120 ft-lbs, making sure that the trigger wheel remains properly aligned with the TDC mark on the crank pulley. A straight-handled box end wrench—like the fan pulley wrench from your tool kit—is preferable to an offset handled combination wrench. The offset variety will put a bending load on the bolt and may deflect it into an out-of-round condition. Applying 200 lbs force to the end of a 7" long fan pulley wrench will provide the proper torque.  
   > **CAUTION:** Be aware that some crankshafts are bored/tapped short—check for bolt bottoming out before finalizing installation.  
   *Now replace/tighten fan belt before proceeding further.*

### B. INSTALLING THE TRIGGER WHEEL SENSOR *(See Figures 3 & 4)*

1. Remove lower two bolts in center of motor mount and set aside.
2. Mount Sensor Bracket to front side of engine mount (between motor mount and fan housing) using original bolts, but adding flat washers between bolt heads and aluminum bracket. Hand tighten.
3. Use fore-aft adjustment to center sensor over trigger wheel to within 0.010". Tighten 10mm bolts securing sensor to bracket.
4. Using feeler gauge, set air gap between sensor and tip of trigger wheel teeth to 0.010" ± 0.005". **THIS DIMENSION IS CRITICAL—DON'T FAKE IT!**  
   **SUGGESTIONS:**
   * **a.** Make sure the center of any tooth lines up with TDC mark on fan housing before starting—do **not** put one of the low points between teeth on TDC.
   * **b.** Get adjustment close, and then put about 5 ft-lbs on one engine mount/bracket bolt. Leave the other bolt hand tight.
   * **c.** Get the final adjustment in spec by moving the loose side.
   * **d.** Check for out-of-round condition by measuring air gap at 90°, 180°, and 270°. If measurements exceed tolerance, go back and review trigger wheel installation procedures.
5. Tighten engine mount/bracket bolts to 35 ft-lbs and recheck air gap. Adjust as required.
6. Route the short gray sensor cable lead rearward through the cutout in the center of the engine mount. *(PROCEED TO SECTION IV)*

## III. SENSOR INSTALLATION FOR 911SC/930 WITH AIR CONDITIONING (DF-3 & DF-4)

### A. PREPARE CAR AND REMOVE ENGINE COMPONENTS FOR ACCESS

1. Disconnect battery, jack up rear of car and put on stands (low setting is OK).
2. Remove OEM ignition components including coil, CD box, distributor and plug wires. *(For 930, remove intercooler and turbo inlet pipe.)*
3. Transfer rubber O-ring from distributor shaft to machined plug supplied with kit and install in distributor hole.
4. Loosen three or four bolts holding AC compressor on mounting platform and belt.
5. Remove AC compressor from platform and carefully swing onto right rear fender after laying down thick pad to protect paint.
6. Remove two top bolts holding AC compressor support bracket to platform—leave bottom bolt that attaches bracket to engine mount.
7. Loosen upper fan belt pulley half and remove fan belt.
8. Remove muffler. *(For 930, remove turbo unit too.)*
9. Remove 10mm bolts holding sheet metal pan at rear of engine and remove rear sheetmetal pan (just the piece under the motor mount, not the whole pan).
10. Disconnect main engine wiring plug from left side fuse/relay panel.
11. Put jack under engine to take load off rear motor mount and remove rear motor mount including cross member and engine bracket.  
    > **NOTE:** You may have to lower the engine on the jack slightly to provide clearance for crossmember removal.
12. With motor mount out of the way, you can now remove the OEM crank pulley.

---

### B. INSTALL DFI SENSOR AND NEW CRANK PULLEY/TRIGGER WHEEL ASSEMBLY *(See Figure 5)*

1. Position drill template in crotch between fan housing and engine case just aft of distributor hole. "V" notch on top of template will match shape of an external rib in the case when radius at bottom of template is laid on outside of #8 main bearing.
2. With template in position, center punch case for drilling sensor bracket mounting holes. Inspect location of punch marks—they must be in the external rib, **NOT** in an area that will put drill holes into the main body of the case.

> **NOTE:** This kit was designed for 1978 and newer cases. Earlier cars will require some machine work on case for trigger wheel clearance.

## V. SETTING ADVANCE CURVE AND REV LIMIT

> **NOTE:** Only **ONE** of these devices—boost retard **OR** vacuum advance—can be installed at a time. Both devices require the sensing of manifold pressure in a plenum shared by all cylinders.

### C. SETTING THE ADVANCE CURVE AND REV LIMIT

1. Remove cover plate at cable end of DFI unit using a small flat blade screwdriver.
2. Dial in each of the three advance settings controlled by knobs on the unit, remembering that they are cumulative.  
   > **CAUTION:** If you have installed your trigger wheel in a static advance position, don't forget to consider that amount when dialing-in your dynamic advance curve.
3. Dial rev limit knob to a setting 500 RPM over planned shift points.

1. Some sample advance curves for various general engine configurations are presented on the following pages.
2. While these curves have proven effective in **OUR** experience, they may not be appropriate for **YOUR** installation.

Don't be afraid to experiment, **BUT** listen and look for signs of detonation.

## VI. PRE-START CHECKLIST

Before starting engine, review the following checklist to assure that you haven't missed anything. Verify that:

* **A.** Trigger wheel is installed so that two dots designating TDC are visible when viewed from rear.
* **B.** Trigger wheel is properly indexed to crank pulley TDC timing mark to give desired static (cranking) advance.
* **C.** Crank trigger sensor is centered over trigger wheel—air gap set to 0.010" ± 0.005". No iron filings or other foreign matter are present in sensor/trigger wheel air gap. Clean as necessary.
* **D.** Trigger wheel and sensor bracket are torqued down per specs.
* **E.** Sensor cable is securely plugged in and routed away from spark plug wires and rotating parts. Use wire ties where appropriate.
* **F.** Primary wiring is connected per instructions with no ballast resistors in the line.
* **G.** Spark plug gaps are set between 0.035" and 0.045" depending on engine configuration and intended use:
  * High compression naturally aspirated (10–12:1) or Turbo: 0.035" - 0.040"
  * Low compression naturally aspirated: 0.040" - 0.045"  
  *In any event, do not extend the gap so far that the angle between center and ground electrodes goes much beyond 90 degrees.*
* **H.** Metal core, spiral wound resistance plug wires and resistance plug connectors are used in wiring harness to prevent electrical noise—**NOT** braided metal sleeves.
* **I.** Plug wires are connected to DFI coils as described in Section IIE (IV.C). **CHECK CAREFULLY, IT'S VERY EASY TO GET THEM OUT OF ORDER.** Plug wires from the same coil pair must **NOT** run parallel.
* **J.** A conservative advance curve appropriate for your engine configuration is dialed in. (See guidelines above.)
* **K.** Previous ignition is disabled by disconnecting power lead to CD box.

> **CAUTION:** Do **NOT** crank engine with plug wires disconnected—damage to DFI will result! If you need to turn the engine over without firing the plugs (e.g., when testing compression), first disconnect the power lead to the DFI unit or unplug the sensor.  
> **Do not arc weld on chassis without first disconnecting BIG RED and BIG BLACK leads!**

## VII. AFTER STARTUP

### A. VERIFY TIMING WITH DIGITAL VOLTMETER (PREFERRED METHOD)

The most accurate means of verifying the advance curve is through use of a digital voltmeter. All timing lights are subject to lag due to the hysteresis of their circuitry. This lag might be as little as 5 microseconds or as much as 500 microseconds depending on the design. Typically, the lag is insensitive to RPM, which means that the higher the RPM, the greater the error in crankshaft degrees.  
*EXAMPLE: A 100 microsecond lag equates to an error of 0.7° at idle and 3.6° at 6000 RPM. Voltmeters are not subject to this problem.*

1. Connect the plus and minus terminals of your digital voltmeter to the **"ADV"** and **"GND"** pins of the DFI unit respectively.
2. Set the meter to a low voltage scale—the DFI output will be in hundredths of a volt.
3. Take voltage readings at **IDLE**, **3000**, and **6000 RPM**. Convert these readings to degrees dynamic advance using the formula:  
   $$\text{0.01 Volts} = 1.0^\circ\text{ Advance}$$
4. Add in your **STATIC** advance setting to obtain the total advance at each RPM point and compare to your dial settings as in A1 to A3 above.
5. For twin plug installations, the two advance curves can be synchronized by connecting the voltmeter between the "ADV" pins of the two DFI units. So connected, the meter will measure the difference between the two—a zero reading equals perfect synch.

---

### B. VERIFY TIMING WITH TIMING LIGHT (OPTIONAL METHOD)

1. Check timing on #1 cylinder at idle (1000–1250 RPM). Advance indicated should equal the sum of "STATIC" and "INITIAL" settings you dialed-in ± 2°.
2. Check timing at 3000 RPM. Advance indicated should be:
   $$\text{"STATIC"} + \text{"INITIAL"} + \text{"3000"}$$
3. After engine has been brought to operating temperature, check timing at 6000 RPM. Advance indicated should be as follows:
   $$\text{"STATIC"} + \text{"INITIAL"} + \text{"3000"} + \left(\frac{3}{5} \times \text{"8000"}\right)$$
4. If timing light readings are more than 1 or 2 degrees off from the dialed-in settings, review the procedures above for aligning the trigger wheel and setting the advance curve.
5. For twin plug installations, use the timing light to verify that both systems are tracking in synch. This can be accomplished by taking readings at idle, 3000, and 6000 RPM—first on the #1 top plug lead, and next on the #1 bottom plug lead.

## VIII. TROUBLESHOOTING

### A. ENGINE TURNS OVER, BUT WON'T FIRE AT ALL

1. With ignition switch ON, measure voltage between **BIG RED** and **BIG BLACK** wires. Then measure voltage between **BIG RED** wire and chassis ground. In both cases, the reading should be between 12.0 and 16.0 volts *(marginal note: 13.47V)*. If not, recheck wiring and/or condition of battery. Also, look for ballast resistor in power feed.
2. With ignition switch OFF, check sensor/trigger wheel air gap. It should be 0.010" ± 0.005" *(marginal note: .005 to .010)*. Gaps down to zero may work—gaps over 0.020" will not. Inspect for metal shavings stuck to magnetic sensor. Remove contamination.
3. With engine cranking, check voltage at **BIG RED** wire. The reading should be at least 10.0 volts for good starting *(marginal note: 10.34V)*. If less than 8 or 9 volts, the system probably won't fire at all. Check battery condition and recheck wiring against instructions.
4. With ignition switch OFF, unplug sensor cable and measure resistance between **RED** and **BLACK** leads to sensor. The reading should be approximately 700 ohms. If it is zero ohms, the wires are shorted; if it is infinity, there is a break in one of the leads. In either case, the sensor/cable assembly must be replaced.
5. With ignition switch ON, check voltage between **"+5V"** output and ground. It should read 5 volts. Now with the ignition switch OFF, check resistance between **"+5V"** output and ground. It should read 1100 ohms ± 200 *(marginal note: 1200)*. If you get any other readings, the DFI unit is defective and must be replaced.
6. With ignition switch OFF, disconnect the **BIG RED** power lead to the DFI unit and measure the resistance between it and the **BIG BLACK** lead. The reading should be approximately 9,000–10,000 ohms *(marginal note: 9600)*. If not, the DFI unit is defective—call us.
7. Are you using Bosch platinum tip plugs? *(handwritten note: "or NGK")* We have experienced some problems with these plugs shorting their internal air gap due to their inability to handle the high energy output of DFI. If this happens, it acts like a shorted plug wire, and can burn out the DFI power transistor. Try using standard tip Bosch plugs, or Nippondenso plugs of either platinum or conventional tips.
8. If you have a twin plug installation and neither unit is firing at all, try disconnecting the **BIG RED** power lead to one of the DFI units. If the engine still won't fire, reconnect that unit and disconnect the other. It is possible for one burned out DFI unit to draw down the other if they are connected to the same power lead. This procedure will identify such a condition.
